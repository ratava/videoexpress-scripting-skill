# VE 3.5 multishot prompt: structure and template

VideoExpress 3.5 image-to-video prompts are written as one paragraph of tagged sections. The official Create Mode examples all follow this order:

`[REFERENCE USE]` → `[IDENTITY / CONTINUITY]` → `[SCENE]` → `[ACTION t-ts]` → `[CAMERA]` → `[LIGHT AND IMAGE]` → `[PRODUCTION SOUND t-ts]` → `[NEGATIVES]`

For longer clips, repeat the `[ACTION]` → `[CAMERA]` → `[LIGHT AND IMAGE]` → `[PRODUCTION SOUND]` group once per segment (e.g. `[ACTION 0-4s]` … `[PRODUCTION SOUND 0-4s]`, then `[ACTION 4-10s]` … `[PRODUCTION SOUND 4-10s]`), each with its own camera, lighting and sound, and each sound block labelled with its segment's time range. **Never put a single [PRODUCTION SOUND] after the last segment:** VE binds it to that segment only and improvises sound, often music, for the earlier ones. Moving to one sound block per segment removed music that had appeared in earlier multi-segment clips (five takes clean). `[NEGATIVES]` comes once at the end.

**First: the clip reference.** When working from a prompt pack, every video prompt (clip, test variant, and the Lipsync HD dialog's Video Prompt) starts with the reference the pack declares for that clip, in square brackets: `[Clip 12]`, `[Clip 12 TEST 1]`. Copy it exactly as the pack writes it, with no other text before it. Library tiles show the first words of the prompt, so this is what matches each tile and take to the pack (Lipsync HD tiles show VE's own caption instead, so identify those by position). A short description may follow the reference if it helps (`[Clip 12] Harbour Run, the landing.`). The reference is a structural label like `[ACTION 0-5s]`. For quick one-off prompts outside a pack, a reference is optional. **Image prompts (start images and Consistent Character references) carry no reference:** VE paints it onto signs, boards and shirts in the image. Rename the saved image tile to its reference instead (see `bible.md`, Start images).

**Custom Creative mode styles: style anchor before [REFERENCE USE].** For a style that isn't a preset, open the prompt (straight after the clip reference) with one sentence naming the medium and its look, ending "drawn and painted, not rendered" (or the equivalent for the medium). Without it, clips drift toward realism. See `styles/README.md`.

## What each section does

**[REFERENCE USE]** — tell the model the supplied image is the start image, the exact opening frame. A clip gets only that image: Consistent Character references are for Create Image (see `bible.md`), so a video prompt names no other reference images.
- Fresh clip: "Use the supplied image as the exact opening frame and visual reference for a ten-second continuous shot, including its drawing style."
- Chained clip: "Use the supplied image, the last frame of the previous shot, as the exact opening frame for a ten-second continuous shot."
- The official 3D example frames it as control: the opening frame "controls the composition, the world, the lighting and the exact framing of this shot".

**[IDENTITY / CONTINUITY]** — who is in the shot and what must not change.
- Paste the character core block and the scene's outfit block verbatim.
- State ownership and exclusivity: "[Name] owns this frame. Characters present: …", "[Name] is the only person wearing [signature accessory]", "Exactly one continuous shot."
- List the props and set pieces to hold in position (official examples list them explicitly: "keep the cabinets, awning, marquee… in their original positions").
- For objects/props in a hand: state contact ("keep the cloth in contact with his fingers").

**[SCENE]** — the environment, in full. Paste the location block, including its out-of-frame sentence (video prompts only; start-image prompts leave it out, see `bible.md`), plus time of day and any sky/weather direction rules. Optional: a line naming the dramatic beat of the shot ("The dramatic beat of this shot: …"). The official 3D example uses this, but in climax scenes emotional framing like this can invite a music score in the generated audio; leave it out when the clip has production sound.

**[ACTION t-ts]** — what happens, in real time, sequenced with words ("In the very first frames… Then… In the final moments…"). Keep it to what the clip length can hold: the official examples use **one small, deliberate action per three seconds** (a head turn, one wipe of a cloth, one rowing pull). For a ten-second clip, two or three segments each with one clear beat work best. State what stays still ("his feet and hanging arm remain planted").

**[CAMERA]** — one explicit move per segment, stated absolutely: "One move only: slow steady push in." "One very slow straight push toward the boy." Add "Subject motion and camera motion are separate; hold the rest of the frame steady." Include the end framing ("ends on a medium-wide shot of her full body") and how it holds ("then holds on that framing"); camera reversing goes in [NEGATIVES]. Keep each move from enlarging a face or prop beyond the size the opening frame shows it (see camera-and-motion.md).

**[LIGHT AND IMAGE]** — lighting direction and colour, then the style line. Official examples add style-stability phrases: "painted details remain stable rather than boiling or crawling", "maintain coherent square pixel clusters with no smoothing or texture crawling", "materials stay tactile and stable".

**[PRODUCTION SOUND t-ts]** — the segment's generated audio, one block per segment, straight after that segment's [LIGHT AND IMAGE] and labelled with the same time range. List short, distinct, unpitched sounds in order and fill the segment's duration: "One heavy boot thud on stone, then one short metallic click as she picks up the case." Name only what is heard, and close the block with it ("Only her footsteps and the click of the case are heard, close-miked"). **Never ask for silence:** a segment whose sound is "silence" renders as low rumble and crackle. Keep pauses under a second, give any quiet stretch a soft sound tied to an action (cloth shifting, a boot settling), and fit the clip length to the action and speech. Every sound exclusion goes in [NEGATIVES]; written inside this block, "no hum" or "no music" is read as a request.
- Sustained ambience (lapping water, wind, hum, whir) renders as a continuous noise floor, so avoid it unless the user wants it.
- Pitched sounds (beeps, rising tones) can turn into music.
- Music, hum and ambience exclusions go in [NEGATIVES] only ("No music, no hum, no background noise"); see `light-sound-dialogue.md`, Sound.
- Dialogue is not repeated here. A character's quoted line lives once, in [ACTION], with explicit lip movement and a voice description (see `light-sound-dialogue.md`, Dialogue); the sound block of the segment where it is spoken refers to it ("his line above in his own on-camera voice, matching his lip movements exactly") without quoting it again, because VE counts quoted speech against the clip length. An off-camera or mouthless character's voice is the exception: write that voice and its one short line here.

**[NEGATIVES]** — the only section where exclusions work. Everywhere else VE reads "no", "not" and "never" phrases as requests, so every exclusion, including sound, lives here. Write each item with "No" ("No music, no hum, no cuts"): in testing, a bare list ("music, hum, rumble") in this section added the listed sounds. Keep it a short tail, not the main control. State the intended behaviour positively in [IDENTITY / CONTINUITY], [SCENE] and [CAMERA] first (what stays still, what stays held, where the camera ends); then a single list starting "No cuts…" (or "Avoid: …") of the specific guards that matter for this clip. When the prompt nears the box limit of about 6,000 characters, this is the first section to trim. Include: cuts, new characters, extra limbs, face or costume drift, style conversion (e.g. "no photorealistic conversion", "no transformation into clay or live action"), unwanted motion (walking, gestures, moving props), text, logos, watermark, plus project-specific guards (camera reversing, cloned crowd, darkening).

## Hard-cut multi-shot variant

One generation can hold two or three shots joined by hard cuts. Declare it in [IDENTITY / CONTINUITY] ("This ten-second generation is a sequence of two shots joined by one hard cut, exactly as described below."), label each segment "Shot one." / "Shot two.", start each later [CAMERA] with "Hard cut to…", and write "no cuts other than the hard cut described" in [NEGATIVES]. The last shot's framing is what a chained clip inherits.

```
[Clip <N>] [REFERENCE USE] … opening frame for this ten-second generation … [IDENTITY / CONTINUITY] <blocks>. This ten-second generation is a sequence of two shots joined by one hard cut, exactly as described below. [SCENE] <location>. [ACTION 0-4s] Shot one. From the very first frame, <action>. [CAMERA] One move only: <move>. [LIGHT AND IMAGE] <light>. <style line>. [PRODUCTION SOUND 0-4s] <shot one's sounds, tied to its action>. [ACTION 4-10s] Shot two. <action>, still <state> in the very last frame. [CAMERA] Hard cut to <framing>; the camera holds still. [LIGHT AND IMAGE] <light>. <style line>. [PRODUCTION SOUND 4-10s] <shot two's sounds, tied to its action>. [NEGATIVES] No cuts other than the hard cut described, <guards>, <sound exclusions: no music, no hum, no background noise>.
```

## Quotation marks

Inside a prompt, quotation marks are reserved for words a character speaks, each line quoted once. The engine lip-syncs and voices anything in quotes and counts it toward the dialogue-length check, so camera phrases, sound descriptions, signage and emphasis are written in plain prose. The quoted fragments in this file are for the reader; drop the quotes when pasting. Two speakers per clip at most; name the speaker right before the quote, delivery and gestures outside it.

## Language

**Every prompt is written in English**, whatever language the user works in: image prompts and video prompts, with or without speech. That covers the section tags, bible blocks, style anchors and style lines, action, camera, sound and negatives. VE's model doesn't reliably understand other languages. VE support confirmed (November 2026) that VE translates a non-English prompt into English before generating, and that translation can change word order and swap nouns; since VE draws the nouns, a changed noun changes what is drawn. In extended testing, prompts written wholly or partly in another language were inconsistent on actions and on speech (two speakers merged into one voice, lines dropped or given to the wrong mouth), and the same prompts in English worked. Ruled out along the way: the order of the voices' pitches, guillemets (« ») instead of plain quotes, open mouths in the start image, and the wording of the voice descriptions. An earlier result (a fully French two-line prompt, 3 of 3 takes) didn't hold up under that testing. Partial translation doesn't work either: no translated tags, no translated negatives, no translated bible blocks, and a clip without speech is in English like any other.

**Only spoken lines are in the user's language**, in plain double quotes, each with "speaking [language] as a native [language] speaker" in the speaker's voice description (`light-sound-dialogue.md`, Other languages). Lipsync HD works the same way: the lines in the user's language go in the Actor Script fields, and the dialog's Video Prompt is English (French lines tested fine).

**The conversation stays in the user's language.** Questions, the script, the pack's notes, each prompt's header and settings line, and the explanation of what a prompt does are in the user's language; only the text inside the prompt code blocks is English. Write the bible blocks in English, once, and paste them word for word; describe each one in the user's language beside it for sign-off. Tell the user once, in their language, why the prompts are in English. If they ask for the prompts in their own language, explain this instead of switching.

**Translated copy for reading (when the user asked for it).** A user who doesn't work in English is asked once whether they want a translated copy shown beside each prompt (SKILL.md, Translated copy), so they can check what a prompt does before using it. When the answer is yes, every image and clip prompt shown in chat is shown twice:

1. The English prompt first, in its code block, exactly as it goes into VE. This is the only version to paste.
2. Below it, a short warning line in bold, in the user's language, meaning "Do not use this prompt in VideoExpress. Use the English version above." (in French: **Ne pas utiliser ce prompt dans VideoExpress. Utilisez la version anglaise ci-dessus.**), then a faithful translation of the whole prompt into the user's language, as a quote block, not a code block, so it can't be mistaken for something to copy. Translate everything, tags included; spoken lines are already in the user's language and stay word for word.

The translation is for reading only: it never goes into VE, into the browser or into a Lipsync HD field, and the pack file keeps the English prompts only unless the user asks for the copies there too. When a prompt is revised, show both versions of the revised prompt in full. If the user spots a problem in the translated copy, fix the English prompt and show both again.

## Text-bearing objects

VE garbles text it has to invent, but keeps lettering the start image already shows: in testing, a carved sign in the start frame held letter for letter until it left the shot. So lettering comes from the start image, never from the clip prompt.

- **A sign, board or label whose wording matters:** name the wording in the start-image prompt (a green neon sign reading OPEN 24 HOURS), drawn large and well inside the frame, and check the candidate's spelling before accepting it (`browser-workflow.md`, Lettering in a start image). In the clip prompt, don't spell or quote the words: say the sign "keeps exactly the lettering shown in the opening frame", and put "No new text, no changing or morphing letters" in [NEGATIVES]. Lettering at the frame edge, or small lettering that a push-in enlarges, garbles: keep the sign at the size and place the start image drew it.
- **Text the clip would have to invent** (a screen whose content changes, a hologram, a sign that comes into view during the clip, a sign the start image shows blank): describe it as abstract shapes and light, with no letters or numbers, and end [NEGATIVES] with "no text, no letters, no numbers".

Numbers elsewhere in the prompt (ages, lengths, counts) can be written as digits or words: in testing, digits there produced no numerals in the frame with those negatives in place.

## Blank template

```
[Clip <N>] [REFERENCE USE] Use the supplied image as the exact opening frame and visual reference for a <length>-second continuous shot. [IDENTITY / CONTINUITY] <character core block> <outfit block> <exclusivity lines>. Preserve her exactly as the opening frame throughout. [SCENE] <location block>. <time of day>. <Name> <position in frame>. [ACTION 0-5s] From the very first frame, <subject action in real time>. <background action>. [CAMERA] <one explicit move with direction and end framing>. [LIGHT AND IMAGE] <lighting>. <style line>. [PRODUCTION SOUND 0-5s] <short unpitched sounds in order, each tied to the action that makes it>. <what is heard in total>. [ACTION 5-10s] <subject keeps moving: specific moves>, still <moving> in the very last frame. [CAMERA] <move continues or settles; end framing>. [LIGHT AND IMAGE] <lighting>. <style line>. [PRODUCTION SOUND 5-10s] <short unpitched sounds in order, each tied to the action that makes it>. <what is heard in total>. [NEGATIVES] No cuts, <project guards>, <sound exclusions: no music, no ambient sound, no background noise, no hiss, no hum>, no text, no letters, no numbers, no logos, no watermark.
```

## Worked generic example (cinematic photoreal, chained, 10 s)

```
[Clip 7] [REFERENCE USE] Use the supplied image, the last frame of the previous shot, as the exact opening frame for a ten-second continuous shot. [IDENTITY / CONTINUITY] Mara, a woman in her late twenties with a lean, athletic build, olive skin, dark brown eyes and a calm, focused expression. Her black hair is always cut in a sharp chin-length bob. Mara wears a fitted charcoal hoodie with the hood down, black tapered joggers, and white high-top sneakers. Mara is the only person in the scene. Preserve her exactly as the opening frame throughout. [SCENE] A long wooden boardwalk beside a calm sea at sunset, lined on the left by a low white rope rail and on the right by a row of closed beach huts painted in faded pastel colours. Soft clouds always travel directly away from the camera toward the horizon, never sideways. Mara skates along the centre of the boardwalk, seen full body. [ACTION 0-5s] From the very first frame, Mara is already skating steadily toward the camera in real time, one foot pushing in a smooth, even rhythm, her arms loose at her sides, her bob swaying gently with her movement. [CAMERA] Tracks backward along the boardwalk at her speed, low and level, keeping her full body centred. [LIGHT AND IMAGE] Warm golden sunset light from the left, soft reflections on the wood. Crisp cinematic photorealistic look, vivid saturated colour, natural skin and fabric detail, sharp focus, stable colour and exposure throughout. [PRODUCTION SOUND 0-5s] The short rhythmic clack of skateboard wheels over the boardwalk seams, then one soft scuff each time she pushes. Only the wheels and her push are heard, close-miked. [ACTION 5-10s] Mara stops pushing and rolls smoothly, turning her head toward the sea, still rolling forward in the very last frame. [CAMERA] Keeps tracking backward at the same speed, then eases to a gentle stop on a medium-wide shot of her full body. [LIGHT AND IMAGE] The same warm light, her face clearly lit. Crisp cinematic photorealistic look, vivid saturated colour, natural skin and fabric detail, sharp focus, stable colour and exposure throughout. [PRODUCTION SOUND 5-10s] The softer, slowing clack of the wheels over the seams as she rolls on, and the light flap of her hoodie. Only the wheels and her hoodie are heard, close-miked. [NEGATIVES] No cuts, no camera reversing, no music, no ambient sound, no background noise, no hiss, no wind noise, no hum, no voices, no other people, no change to Mara's face, hair or outfit, no slow motion, no jerky movement, no ending closer than her full body, no text, no letters, no numbers, no logos, no watermark.
```
