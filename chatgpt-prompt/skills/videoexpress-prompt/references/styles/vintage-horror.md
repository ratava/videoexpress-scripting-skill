# Vintage Horror (eerie black-and-white antique photograph and old film)

**Type**: custom, tested in production (pass)

- **Medium phrase**: "Eerie antique black-and-white photograph from the early 1900s, heavy film grain, faded silver tones, soft vignette, scratches and dust, deep shadows and a single dim light source, widescreen 16:9." Video anchor: "Eerie black-and-white silent-film footage from the early 1900s: heavy flickering film grain, faded silver tones, dust and scratches, a soft dark vignette, deep shadows and dim light, slow unsettling movement, slight frame-rate stutter, an old film, not a modern colour video."
- **Character design**: period figures (Victorian or Edwardian dress, nightgowns, high collars) with pale faces and dark eyes, often still or posed; faces soft and slightly blurred in low light; nothing grotesque needed, the unease comes from stillness and shadow.
- **Materials / rendering**: monochrome only, warm or cool silver tone; strong grain, dust specks, hair-line scratches, uneven exposure and soft lens vignetting; deep blacks with little detail; old interiors with peeling wallpaper, dark wood, tall clocks, staircases into darkness.
- **Light**: one weak source (a candle, a window, a lantern) with heavy falloff into black; soft glow around flames.
- **Motion**: slow and unsettling: a candle flame flickering, a head turning slowly toward the camera, a door creaking open on its own, a pendulum swinging, the film itself flickering; locked camera or a very slow push.
- **Stability line**: "Strictly an eerie early-1900s black-and-white photograph and silent film, heavy film grain, dust and scratches, faded silver tones, soft vignette, deep shadows, no colour at any point, stable exposure apart from gentle film flicker, no conversion to modern colour video."
- **Guards**: no colour, no sepia beyond a faint silver tone, no modern clean digital look, no gore, no monsters unless asked, no 3D rendering, no cartoon or illustration look, no other text, no watermark.
- **Artistly match**: Creepy Vintage.
- **Known failures**: none in the test (a Victorian girl with a candle in an abandoned mansion hallway, a VINTAGE HORROR sign): monochrome, grain, vignette and the sign all held. Risks still worth watching: colour creeping in, the grain and scratches disappearing into a clean digital look, and the figure's face changing in the low light.
